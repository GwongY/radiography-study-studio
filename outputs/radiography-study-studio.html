AP and PA — written by this app, not from your sources

The name is the direction the beam travels through the patient. PA is posterior → anterior: the patient faces the detector and the beam enters the back. AP is anterior → posterior: the beam enters the front and the detector is behind.

What actually differs is geometry. Whatever sits further from the detector is magnified more and blurred more. That is the whole difference — and it is the part this view reproduces, because the beam here diverges from a real source distance. Switch between PA and AP and watch which structures grow.

Why it matters on a chest. The heart is an anterior structure. On PA it lies almost against the detector and projects close to its true size. On AP it sits further away and comes out magnified — which is why heart size is not judged from an AP film.

Why AP gets used at all. It does not need the patient to stand and face a detector, so it is the ward, mobile and trauma projection.

Both are hung the same way — as though you are facing the patient, their right on your left. That is why this view mirrors the PA image and not the AP one.

One thing this view does not show: real PA chest positioning rolls the shoulders forward so the scapulae swing clear of the lung fields, while on an AP film they overlie them. That is patient positioning, not beam direction — the model cannot move its arms, so the scapulae sit identically in both here. Do not learn that difference from this picture.

Your supplied set names only PA and Lat, in one worked chest request form, and never contrasts them with AP. Everything in this box is therefore app-authored study scaffolding rather than a claim traced to a source file, and is labelled as such under the same rule the rest of the app follows.

Simulated projection, not a radiograph. Each ray's path length through material is measured and attenuated by Beer–Lambert, with bone about ten times the coefficient of soft tissue, so bone is bright because the ray spent longer in bone. The beam diverges from a point at a set source-to-image distance, which is what makes PA and AP genuinely differ here: whatever lies further from the detector magnifies more.

What it still is not. The coefficients are relative, chosen for familiar contrast — no kVp, no mAs, no dose. There is no scatter, no grid, no pathology. And the source meshes are hollow surfaces, so cortex and marrow do not differ: a long bone reads as uniformly dense where a real film shows a bright cortex around a darker medullary cavity.

Use it for projection anatomy — what overlaps what, and where a structure lands when the body is flattened onto a detector. It will not teach you to read a film.

Real images go in assets/xray/, which is scaffolded and empty. Drop a licence-cleared file there and reference it from a question to have it render.

Progress transfer

Move progress between devices

There is no sync, and no server. Mastery is stored in this browser alone. Export writes a JSON file; import reads one back on the other device.
Import
How merging decides. For each item and dimension the whole record with the more recent activity wins — records are never recombined field by field. Attempt counts are cumulative, and with no event log there is no way to know whether two devices logged the same attempt or two different ones, so adding them would inflate your history and taking the larger would invent a total neither device reached. The older side of a clash is dropped rather than guessed at. Mistakes are timestamped, so those genuinely merge.
Erase

Reset progress on this device

This cannot be undone. There is no server and no sync, so this browser is the only place your history exists. Once it is gone it is gone, unless you exported a file first.

    Sources / architecture

    Seven named anatomical layers

    Active model: seven Z-Anatomy / BodyParts3D GLB layers, about 2,900 individually named and selectable meshes in one shared coordinate frame — skeleton (277), muscles (683), ligaments and joints (413), organs (120), vessels (676), nerves (582) and lymphatic (163). The skeleton loads first; the other six are fetched the moment you first switch one on, so a first install does not download neuroanatomy for someone studying bones.

    Model attribution

    BodyParts3D / Anatomography, © The Database Center for Life Science, licensed under CC BY-SA 2.1 JP. The layer set is distributed through the open anatomi-simulatoru project, which documents the per-structure BodyParts3D source and the share-alike attribution.

    Spatial overlays are measured, not drawn

    The body cavities, the nine abdominopelvic regions and the four quadrants are not in any GLB. They are generated at runtime from the loaded meshes: the thoracic cavity is swept from the inner surface of these ribs, sternum and thoracic vertebrae and floored by the diaphragm; the vertebral canal is threaded through the foramen found in each vertebra; the pelvic cavity is the space inside these hip bones below the brim. Nothing is placed by hand, so the overlays follow the model rather than a remembered position, and they would follow a different body too.

    Where a cavity is defined by a structure from a layer you have not loaded — the pericardial sac is defined by the heart — the viewer falls back to the strongest bony reference it does have and says on the card that the result is estimated rather than measured.

    Legacy files

    assets/bodyparts3d/ still holds five per-bone upper-limb GLBs (clavicle, scapula, humerus, radius, ulna) from an earlier iteration of this app. They are not the active source and nothing in the current viewer is built around them.

    Offline and failure behaviour

    Anatomy search, records, regions and quiz choices render without the 3D dependency. If the local assets or the three.js CDN are unavailable, the model area exits with a retryable fallback state instead of leaving an infinite spinner.
