      AP and PA — written by this app, not from your sources

      The name is the direction the beam travels through the patient. PA is posterior → anterior: the patient faces the detector and the beam enters the back. AP is anterior → posterior: the beam enters the front and the detector is behind.

      What actually differs is geometry. Whatever sits further from the detector is magnified more and blurred more. That is the whole difference — and it is the part this view reproduces, because the beam here diverges from a real source distance. Switch between PA and AP and watch which structures grow.

      Why it matters on a chest. The heart is an anterior structure. On PA it lies almost against the detector and projects close to its true size. On AP it sits further away and comes out magnified — which is why heart size is not judged from an AP film.

      Why AP gets used at all. It does not need the patient to stand and face a detector, so it is the ward, mobile and trauma projection.

      Both are hung the same way — as though you are facing the patient, their right on your left. That is why this view mirrors the PA image and not the AP one.

      One thing this view does not show: real PA chest positioning rolls the shoulders forward so the scapulae swing clear of the lung fields, while on an AP film they overlie them. That is patient positioning, not beam direction — the model cannot move its arms, so the scapulae sit identically in both here. Do not learn that difference from this picture.

      Your supplied set names only PA and Lat, in one worked chest request form, and never contrasts them with AP. Everything in this box is therefore app-authored study scaffolding rather than a claim traced to a source file, and is labelled as such under the same rule the rest of the app follows.

      Simulated projection, not a radiograph. Each ray’s path through material is measured in centimetres and attenuated by Beer–Lambert, using mass attenuation coefficients from the NIST tables at the effective energy your kVp implies. Bone is bright because the ray spent longer in bone. The beam diverges from a point at a set source-to-image distance, which is what makes PA and AP genuinely differ here.

      kVp changes contrast; mAs changes density and noise. Cortical bone attenuates 8.8× soft tissue at 50 kVp and 3.4× at 125 kVp — that ratio is subject contrast, and it is why kVp is a contrast control rather than a brightness one. mAs and distance set how many photons arrive, so the grain is quantum mottle at 1/√N, while image pan and zoom keep SID fixed. AEC holds detector fluence steady; switch it off to compare manual exposures.

      Why the lungs are dark. Not because they are made of something different — NIST gives lung and soft tissue the same mass attenuation coefficient. An inflated lung is lucent because it is a quarter as dense, 0.26 against 1.06 g/cm³. Density alone.

      What it still is not. A single effective energy stands in for a whole spectrum, so there is no beam hardening. There is no scatter, no grid, no anode heel effect and no pathology. Cortical thickness is uniform at 2 mm, so a rib is drawn with a femur’s cortex. The muscle and organ meshes overlap in places, so some soft tissue is still counted twice. Vessels and nerves are absent from the beam on purpose — a plain film cannot see them without contrast.

      Coefficients are NIST / ICRU reference data, not your course sources

      Sources / architecture

      Seven named anatomical layers

      Active model: seven Z-Anatomy / BodyParts3D GLB layers in one shared body frame, holding named structures — skeleton (), muscles (), ligaments and joints (), organs (), vessels (), nerves () and lymphatic (). They resolve to things a tap can select. The counts on the layer chips are structures, not meshes: the same thing is exported once per side, again per duplicate and again per sub-part, and tapping a sub-part selects the whole it belongs to. of the structures are named somewhere in your own HSS2011 / ABCT2326 material, and the chips lead with that number. Which file names each one is read off the taught and assessed documents by work/build-course-terms.mjs, not decided here. The skeleton loads first; the other six are fetched the moment you first switch one on.

      Which names you are asked to know

      An atlas is not a vocabulary list. Every structure therefore carries the file that names it, or records that nothing does. A build step reads the HSS2011 and ABCT2326 teaching and assessment material and asks, for each structure, whether the course names it. do: are named in running text, appear in a term list or table, are named with an explanation (your notes say “the right lung is divided into superior, middle and inferior lobes”, never “Superior lobe of right lung”), is the mirror image of a structure named on the other side, and finish a numbered series the sources name over and over — a course that names T1, T2, T4, T5, T6 and T12 in six different documents is teaching the thoracic vertebrae, not six of them.

      Three things are left out of that corpus on purpose. The set textbook — Martini, Fundamentals of Anatomy & Physiology — names essentially every structure in the body, so counting it would mark almost everything examinable and destroy the distinction; it remains a source for facts, but it is not evidence that a NAME must be memorised. Student coursework is evidence of topic scope only, which is what the source registry already says. And the publisher’s question bank is not taught material.

      What a tap can select

      Knowing which names matter is only half of it. The model splits further than any course does — the deltoid exists only as three “parts”, the left lung as eleven segmental bronchi, the wrist as thirty-three separately named ligaments — and while every one of those was individually selectable, tapping the wrist answered “Dorsal scaphotriquetral ligament”, a name that appears in no lecture, no exercise and no paper.

      So every structure now resolves to a study unit, and the unit is what a tap selects. A structure the course names is its own unit and keeps its name. Everything below that joins one, in the order the anatomy allows: into the whole it is a piece of, where the model has one (the branches of the ulnar nerve are the ulnar nerve); under the whole they collectively make up, where it has none (the three “parts” become the deltoid); or under their kind and where they are — “Ligaments of the foot”, “Bronchi of the left lung”, “Lymph nodes of the abdomen”. Where they are is measured off the model, not read out of the name: nothing in the words “capitohamate interosseous ligament” says hand, but the nearest bone to it does.

      That gives things a tap can select: the course names, groups, and structures that are genuinely one of a kind with nothing to join. Those stay selectable under their own name and say so. Nothing is hidden — every name is still searchable, and searching one that belongs to a group opens the group and tells you which name it was found under.

      Spatial overlays are measured, not drawn

      The body cavities, the nine abdominopelvic regions and the four quadrants are not in any GLB. They are generated at runtime from the loaded meshes: the thoracic cavity is swept from the inner surface of these ribs, sternum and thoracic vertebrae and floored by the diaphragm; the vertebral canal is threaded through the foramen found in each vertebra; the pelvic cavity is the space inside these hip bones below the brim. Nothing is placed by hand, so the overlays follow the model rather than a remembered position, and they would follow a different body too.

      The nine regions and the four quadrants are not spaces at all — they are topographic divisions, so they are built the way a clinician draws them: two midclavicular lines dropped from the midpoint of each clavicle, the subcostal plane at the inferior border of the tenth costal cartilage, the transtubercular plane through the tubercle of each iliac crest, the median and transumbilical planes for the quadrants. Every one of those is a measured level and stays a flat, straight boundary; only the projection onto the front of the torso curves, because the belly does. The organs underneath are what the regions are used to talk about and are deliberately excluded from the measurement — the liver crosses three regions and is not allowed to bend one.

      Where a cavity is defined by a structure from a layer you have not loaded — the pericardial sac is defined by the heart — the viewer falls back to the strongest bony reference it does have and says on the card that the result is estimated rather than measured.

      Offline and failure behaviour

      Search, records and regions render without the 3D dependency. If the local assets or the three.js CDN are unavailable, the model area exits with a retryable fallback state instead of leaving an infinite spinner.
